# Legal access to Platforms for a paid layer, and EU data protection for athletes' health data

Research for issue #937. Every source below was read on **2026-11-23** unless a line says otherwise. Quotes are verbatim and linked. This document states what the sources say and where they are ambiguous. It is not legal advice and it recommends nothing.

Context: today the Kaiord App reaches each Platform through the athlete's own Session, unofficially, as declared in [ADR 0002](../adr/0002-platform-access-through-the-athletes-session.md) and on the public [Platform Access](../../packages/docs/legal/platform-access.md) page. The question here is what an optional paid "Pro" layer with accounts (managed AI, managed cross-device sync, coach/teams), with athlete data on EU servers only, could use instead, and what EU data protection asks of such a service.

How the sources were read:

- Most pages were downloaded as plain HTML or PDF and quoted from the extracted text.
- EUR-Lex, the TANITA Europe terms (tanita.eu, behind an Anubis bot check) and some EUR-Lex regulations were read through a summarising fetch tool that returns quotes of at most about 125 characters. Those quotes are marked **(fetch-tool excerpt)** and should be checked against the source before anyone relies on them.
- Pages that could not be read are listed in [What could not be read](#what-could-not-be-read).
- No account was used, no programme was applied to, nobody was contacted.

## Garmin Connect

**Programme.** The [Garmin Connect Developer Program overview](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/overview/) now has a Personal track and two Business tracks:

- Personal: "Secure access to your own Garmin Connect data for individuals who want to explore, analyze, and build personalized experiences using their own data." Activity Data (JSON and .FIT), Health Data, Women's Health Data; "Delivery: Pull only".
- Business Starter: "The starting point for organizations building apps with Garmin Connect API access." Activity, Courses, Health, Training and Women's Health APIs; "Delivery: Push only"; "Historical Data: Not included"; "User Capacity: Limited". "Apply for the Garmin Connect Developer Program and create an app with Starter Access."
- Business Enterprise: "Designed for companies operating at scale requiring capabilities beyond Starter Access." Adds the Golf API, "Push or Ping/Pull", "Historical Data: 1 month +", "User Capacity: Unlimited", "Technical Support: Included". "Available options and commercial terms are tailored based on business and technical requirements."

The [Program FAQ](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/enterprise/program-faq/) says: "After you request access to the Garmin Connect Developer Program and your request has been approved, you will be granted access to the Garmin Developer Portal ... A typical integration takes between 1 and 4 weeks." and "All APIs in the Developer Program use OAUTH 2.0." No fee figure appears on any Garmin page read. The request form ([Starter access form](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/forms/StarterGarminConnectDeveloperAccess/)) renders only with JavaScript, so its fields and any entity requirement were not read.

**Personal API.** The [Personal API FAQ](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/personal/personal-faq/): "The Personal API is intended for individual Garmin users who want programmatic access to their own data. It is not intended for commercial applications serving multiple end users." Keys expire after 30, 90, 180 days or one year; access tokens are "valid for 24 hours or until the Personal API key expires"; "refresh tokens are not supported". The Personal API terms are accepted behind a Garmin Connect login and were not read.

**Agreement.** The [Garmin Connect Developer Program Agreement](https://developerportal.garmin.com/sites/default/files/Garmin%20Connect%20Developer%20Program%20Agreement.pdf) (FRM-0952 Rev. B; the PDF's own metadata dates it October 2023) between Garmin International, Inc. and the Licensee:

- §4.1 licence: "embed the API ... solely for internal business purposes" and "use data transmitted through the API (i) solely for internal business purposes, (ii) only to the extent necessary to format and display such data ... and (iii) only as allowed by law and in compliance with each End User's consent."
- §4.2: Garmin "may approve or decline the Licensee Application in its sole discretion" and may "rescind or suspend any License Key, even if the Licensee Application meets the Application Requirements."
- §5.2(e): Licensee shall not "sell, lease, share, transfer, or sublicense the License Key, provide access to the API, or derive income from the use or provision of the API, whether for direct commercial or monetary gain or otherwise, without Garmin's prior written permission".
- §5.2(o): no "developing a Licensee application programming interface to provide End User Data to third-party websites, software applications, platforms, services, or products that have not been approved by Garmin in writing".
- §5.2(t): not "use the API or any Garmin Brand Feature to compete with Garmin".
- §5.1(i)-(j): comply with FDA and other regulation and "only market a Licensee Application for such Licensee Application's cleared or approved intended use".
- §5.1(g)-(h): notify Garmin within 24 hours of security weaknesses and of "any confirmed systems breach, data breach, Personal Data Breach".
- §6.1: "Licensee must include the Garmin Brand Features" when showing Garmin data. The [API Brand Guidelines](https://developer.garmin.com/downloads/brand/Garmin-Developer-API-Brand-Guidelines.pdf): "All uses of Garmin device-sourced data as an input to analytics, algorithms, machine learning models, artificial intelligence or combined, aggregated or blended with other sources ... must include a Garmin attribution."
- §8.4: no press release about Garmin without "Garmin's express prior written approval".
- §9.2: Garmin "may terminate this Agreement at any time by providing Licensee with thirty (30) days' prior written notice."
- §10.1: "no license fees or other payments will be due under this Agreement", but "Garmin reserves the right to charge fees for future use of or access to the API".
- §15.2: "Garmin is an independent 'controller', not Licensee's 'processor'". §15.4: before collecting, provide notice and confirm consent, "provide the End User with the ability to easily withdraw such consent", and honour access, erasure and export requests. §15.8: Licensee "shall not request or otherwise attempt to obtain the End User's Garmin login credentials". §15.9 and Annex A incorporate the Commission SCCs (module one, controller to controller), and list as sensitive data: "Depending on the API used, data exporter may transfer data relating to the health of a data subject." Retention: "For duration of the End User maintaining an account with data importer or until End User exercises rights to delete Personal Data".
- §16.11-16.12: arbitration in New York for non-US licensees; New York law.

**Version uncertainty.** intervals.icu's API page (a third party affected by the change) records: "Update 9th October 2026: Garmin terms and conditions have been updated" and "Update 14th October 2026: The revisions to the agreement are part of the planned reopening of the Garmin Connect Developer Program." ([source](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/api-access-to-intervals-icu/609)). The only other agreement PDF found, at [www8.garmin.com](https://www8.garmin.com/en-US/GARMINCONNECTDEVELOPERPROGRAMAGREEMENT/GARMINCONNECTDEVELOPERPROGRAMAGREEMENT_EN.pdf), is the variant with Garmin China Shanghai RHQ Co., Ltd. and Shanghai courts. It keeps §4.1 and §5.2(e) as above and adds §15.10: the privacy policy "shall include a conspicuous artificial intelligence transparency statement ... if End User Data will be used for training with, or processing by, artificial intelligence systems", including "obtaining explicit consent from each End User before initiating any processing". Whether the current global agreement carries the same clause, and whether §5.2(e) still stands in it, was not read.

**Rate limits.** Not public; §5.2(f) refers to "reasonable request volume" and §10.2 to fees for volume above limits in the Application Requirements.

## WHOOP

**Programme.** [WHOOP Developer Platform](https://developer.whoop.com/docs/introduction). Self-serve: "To start developing with WHOOP, you first need to create an App in the WHOOP Developer Dashboard" ([Getting Started](https://developer.whoop.com/docs/developing/getting-started)); up to 5 apps.

**Approval.** [App Approval](https://developer.whoop.com/docs/developing/app-approval): "Every new app starts on the Sandbox tier, which allows up to 10 WHOOP members to connect." Tiers Sandbox 10, Build 100, Growth 1,000, Scale 5,000, Production unlimited; "From Build onward, moving up requires at least 50% of your current tier's member limit to be connected." "Requests are reviewed manually ... Requests without specific, detailed answers will be rejected." A Privacy Policy URL is required.

**[API Terms of Use](https://developer.whoop.com/api-terms-of-use/)** (effective October 6, 2026):

- §1 Pricing: "Access to and use of the APIs is currently provided at no charge."
- §3 may not "Sell, rent, lease, redistribute, or syndicate access ... provided that Company is not prohibited from charging a fee for the provision of functionality not provided by the WHOOP Platform in Company Applications".
- §3 may not "Market, sell, license or lease data ... even if an End User consents"; "Create any applications ... that function substantially the same as any API"; "Use the APIs to design, develop, augment, or support a medical device ... or a feature or application that would be regulated as a medical device"; "Use the APIs to offer, solicit, obtain, or provide any medical, legal, or other professional advice"; "Use any API Materials to compete, directly or indirectly, with WHOOP".
- §3 Confidentiality: "Developer credentials may not be embedded in open-source projects."
- §4 may not "Scrape, build databases, or otherwise create permanent copies of WHOOP Data, or keep cached copies longer than permitted by the cache header"; may not "Use any API or WHOOP Data to create, develop, test, train, fine-tune or improve any large language model or any other machine learning or artificial intelligence model, application, or system"; may not "Expose WHOOP Data to other users or to third parties without explicit opt-in consent".
- §2: "ensure that any WHOOP Data is encrypted (in transit and at rest)"; notify WHOOP of a Security Incident "within 48 hours".
- §5: no statement "that references WHOOP or the APIs without the prior written approval of WHOOP".
- §7: on termination "delete any cached or stored content". §9: Massachusetts law, Suffolk county courts.

**Data and auth.** Scopes in the [API reference](https://developer.whoop.com/api/): `read:recovery`, `read:cycles`, `read:sleep`, `read:workout`, `read:body_measurement`, `read:profile`; all read. [OAuth 2.0](https://developer.whoop.com/docs/developing/oauth) with Client ID and Client Secret; refresh tokens need the `offline` scope. [Webhooks](https://developer.whoop.com/docs/developing/webhooks) need "an HTTPS URL endpoint" with signature validation. [Rate limits](https://developer.whoop.com/docs/developing/rate-limiting): "100 requests per minute" and "10,000 requests per day", increase on request.

**Ambiguity.** The ban on "permanent copies" and "build databases" sits next to a cache-header limit; how it applies to a user-requested sync store of the athlete's own WHOOP data on our servers is not stated.

## EU data protection

### Is this health data?

- GDPR [Art. 4(15)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng): "'data concerning health' means personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person" (fetch-tool excerpt). Recital 35: "Personal data concerning health should include all data pertaining to the health status of a data subject" (fetch-tool excerpt).
- Article 29 Working Party, [annex to the letter on health data in apps and devices](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/other-document/files/2015/20150205_letter_art29wp_ec_health_data_after_plenary_annex_en.pdf) (5 February 2015): "personal data are health data when: 1. The data are inherently/clearly medical data 2. The data are raw sensor data that can be used in itself or in combination with other data to draw a conclusion about the actual health status or health risk of a person 3. Conclusions are drawn about a person's health status or health risk". It also says some lifestyle data is "in general, not to be regarded as health data".
- Ambiguous: where each data type falls (pace and power versus HRV, sleep, resting HR, weight, body-fat, recovery scores, ECG and SpO2 from Polar Elixir). The criteria above, not a list, decide it.

### Lawful basis: Article 6 plus an Article 9 condition

- Art. 9(1) prohibits processing of special categories, health data among them; Art. 9(2)(a) lifts it where "the data subject has given explicit consent to the processing of those personal data for one or more specified purposes," (fetch-tool excerpt). Art. 9(4): "Member States may maintain or introduce further conditions, including limitations," for health data (fetch-tool excerpt).
- Art. 6(1)(b) (contract) is a basis under Art. 6, but EDPB [Guidelines 05/2020 on consent](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf) v1.1, ¶99: "Article 9(2) does not recognize 'necessary for the performance of a contract' as an exception to the general prohibition to process special categories of data. ... Should none of the exceptions (b) to (j) apply, obtaining explicit consent in accordance with the conditions for valid consent in the GDPR remains the only possible lawful exception to process such data."
- How explicit consent is given, ¶93: "The term explicit refers to the way consent is expressed by the data subject. It means that the data subject must give an express statement of consent." ¶94: online it may be "filling in an electronic form, by sending an email, by uploading a scanned document ... or by using an electronic signature". ¶96: a screen "that contains Yes and No check boxes, provided that the text clearly indicates the consent, for instance 'I, hereby, consent to the processing of my data'". ¶98: two-stage verification.
- Art. 7(3): "The data subject shall have the right to withdraw his or her consent at any time." (fetch-tool excerpt). Recital 32: "Silence, pre-ticked boxes or inactivity should not therefore constitute consent." Recital 43 presumes consent not freely given where "the performance of a contract, including the provision of a service, is dependent on the consent" (fetch-tool excerpts).
- Ambiguous: how consent can be "freely given" (recital 43) when the paid feature itself (managed AI over health data) cannot run without processing that data.
- Platform contracts add their own consent duties: Garmin §15.4 (and the China variant's §15.10 explicit consent for AI), Strava Policy 2.1 and 7.2, WHOOP §2, Polar 3.1.1, Apple DPLA §3.3.3 H, Google Play health policy.

### DPIA, records, DPO

- Art. 35(1): a DPIA where processing "is likely to result in a high risk"; Art. 35(3)(b): in particular "processing on a large scale of special categories of data referred to in Article 9(1)"; Art. 35(4): authorities publish lists; Art. 36(1): prior consultation if high residual risk (fetch-tool excerpts).
- Spain's AEPD [list under Art. 35(4)](https://www.aepd.es/documento/listas-dpia-es-35-4.pdf): a DPIA is needed "en la mayoría de los casos en los que dicho tratamiento cumpla con dos o más criterios". Criteria include 1 (profiling, data "en múltiples ámbitos de su vida"), 3 (systematic monitoring "a través de redes, aplicaciones"), 4 ("categorías especiales de datos a las que se refiere el artículo 9.1"), 7 (large scale), 8 ("asociación, combinación o enlace de registros de bases de datos de dos o más tratamientos con finalidades diferentes o por responsables distintos") and 9 (minors under 14, among other vulnerable subjects).
- Art. 30(5): the under-250-employees exemption from records of processing does not apply where "the processing includes special categories of data as referred to in Article 9(1)" (fetch-tool excerpt).
- Art. 37(1)(c): a DPO where core activities consist of "processing on a large scale of special categories of data pursuant to Article 9" (fetch-tool excerpt). What counts as "large scale" for a small paid layer is ambiguous.

### Processors, sub-processors, controllers

- Art. 28(1): only processors "providing sufficient guarantees"; Art. 28(2): a processor "shall not engage another processor without prior specific or general written authorisation" (fetch-tool excerpts). EDPB [Guidelines 07/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf) v2.1: "In the case of general written authorisation, the processor must inform the controller" of changes, giving "the controller the opportunity to object to such changes".
- Roles the Platforms assign: Garmin "independent 'controller'" (§15.2); Strava "separate and independent controller" (Policy 7.6); Polar: the licensee "shall also be data controller" (5.3); Train2Go: the coach is "responsable del Tratamiento".
- Art. 26(1): "Where two or more controllers jointly determine the purposes and means of processing, they shall be joint controllers." (fetch-tool excerpt). Who is controller when a coach on a Pro team sees an athlete's data is not settled by any source read.
- Art. 32(1)(a) lists "the pseudonymisation and encryption of personal data"; Art. 33(1): notify the authority "not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it" (fetch-tool excerpts). Platform contracts are shorter: Garmin 24 hours, Strava 24 hours, WHOOP 48 hours.
- Art. 22(1) and 22(4): no solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects, and such decisions "shall not be based on special categories" save exceptions (fetch-tool excerpts). Whether any AI feature reaches that threshold is not settled.
- Art. 20(1): data the athlete provided, "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format" (fetch-tool excerpt).

### International transfers and EU-only hosting

- Art. 44: transfers to third countries "shall take place only if" Chapter V conditions are met; Art. 45 adequacy; Art. 46(2)(c) Commission SCCs; Art. 13(1)(f): inform of intended transfers (fetch-tool excerpts).
- EDPB [Recommendations 01/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2021-06/edpb_recommendations_202001vo.2.0_supplementarymeasurestransferstools_en.pdf) v2.0 (18 June 2021): "remote access from a third country (for example in support situations) and/or storage in a cloud situated outside the EEA, is also considered to be a transfer"; "remote access by an entity from a third country to data located in the EEA is also considered a transfer."
- Implication stated by the sources, not concluded here: storing in an EU region does not by itself rule out a transfer if a provider or sub-processor outside the EEA can access the data, for example for support or model inference.
- [Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2023/1795/oj/eng) of 10 July 2023 is the adequacy decision for the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (title read; its operative text and current standing were not read).
- Several Platforms are US companies (Garmin International, WHOOP Inc., TrainingPeaks LLC, Strava Inc. outside the EEA, Wahoo). Garmin's agreement incorporates SCC module one for its own transfers (§15.9, Annex A); Strava Policy 7.6 does the same.

### EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) and the Digital Omnibus on AI

- Art. 3(3) provider: one that "develops an AI system or a general-purpose AI model" or has one developed and places it on the market under its own name; Art. 3(4) deployer: one "using an AI system under its authority" (fetch-tool excerpts, [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689)).
- Art. 50(1) applies to "AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons"; Art. 50(5): information "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure" (fetch-tool excerpts).
- [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng) (Digital Omnibus on AI, 8 July 2026, OJ 24 July 2026), fetch-tool excerpts: new Art. 111(4) providers of generative systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 "shall take the necessary steps in order to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026"; Art. 113 now reads Chapter III Sections 1-3 apply from "2 December 2027 as regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article 6(2) and Annex III" and "2 August 2028 as regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article 6(1) and Annex I"; Art. 4 (AI literacy) is replaced: "Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff". The 2 August 2026 general date of application comes from the original Art. 113, which was not re-read here.
- Art. 6(1): high-risk where the AI system is a safety component of, or is, a product under Annex I legislation "required to undergo a third-party conformity assessment" (fetch-tool excerpt). Annex I and Annex III were beyond the truncated page and were not read; whether an AI coaching feature over health metrics could fall there (for example via medical-device rules) is open.
- Platform terms point at the same law: Strava Policy 5.14 requires compliance with "the regulation of artificial intelligence (including the EU AI Act, where applicable)".

### EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854)

From [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32023R2854) (fetch-tool excerpts) and the Commission's [Data Act explained](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/data-act-explained):

- Art. 2(5) connected product: "an item that obtains, generates or collects data concerning its use or environment"; Art. 2(6) related service: "a digital service, other than an electronic communications service, including software" that affects the product's functions; Art. 2(12) user: the person who owns the connected product or holds rights to use it.
- Art. 4(1): data holders make readily available data accessible to the user. Art. 5(1): "Upon request by a user, or by a party acting on behalf of a user," the data holder makes readily available data available to a third party. Art. 5(3): a gatekeeper "shall not be an eligible third party".
- Art. 6(1): the third party processes "only for the purposes and under the conditions agreed with the user" and erases data when no longer needed. Art. 6(2)(c)-(e): limits on passing data on, no passing to gatekeepers, and not "use the data it receives to develop a product that competes with the connected product."
- Art. 9(1): compensation to the data holder must be "non- discriminatory and reasonable and may include a margin"; Art. 9(4) caps it at cost "Where the data recipient is an SME or a not-for-profit research organisation".
- Art. 50: applies "from 12 September 2025"; the Art. 3(1) design obligation covers products and related services placed on the market "after 12 September 2026".
- Commission page: connected products include "medical and fitness devices"; "data holders must make the data available to users for free"; "there is no obligation under the Data Act for a data holder to share data with third parties based outside the EU"; "Where the user is not the data subject whose data is being requested, personal data can only be made available if there is a valid legal basis (e.g. consent)."
- Seen in Platform terms: TANITA Art. 7 (above) and Polar §13.7. No Data Act request process was found for Garmin, WHOOP, Suunto, COROS or Wahoo in this session.
- Ambiguous: whether apps such as Garmin Connect or WHOOP's app are "related services", which derived metrics are "readily available data", and whether web-only Platforms without a device (TrainingPeaks, Train2Go, intervals.icu, Strava) are in scope at all.

### European Health Data Space (Regulation (EU) 2025/327)

From [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng) (11 February 2025, OJ 5 March 2025; fetch-tool excerpts):

- Art. 2(2)(ab) wellness application: "any software, or any combination of hardware and software, intended by the manufacturer to be used by a natural person" to process health data for purposes other than healthcare.
- Art. 1(2)(b): the rules reach wellness applications "which are claimed to be interoperable with EHR systems"; Art. 47: their label "shall be issued by the manufacturer of the wellness application".
- Art. 51(1)(i) lists "data from wellness applications;" among minimum categories for secondary use.
- Art. 105: the Regulation applies from 26 March 2027; Articles 47-49 follow the staged dates of 26 March 2029 and 26 March 2031; Chapter IV applies from 26 March 2029.
- Ambiguous: whether a paid layer that never claims EHR interoperability is touched by the EHDS at all, and whether it could be a "health data holder" under Chapter IV (that definition was not read).

1. Garmin: what the agreement revised in October 2026 says for the global (non-China) programme; whether §5.2(e) "derive income" still applies, and what "prior written permission" takes; Starter "User Capacity: Limited" in numbers; any fees; whether an EU entity is required.
